01 — Why a market

The rate card hides the market.

Cloud menus were built for commits. A spot market is built for the hour a job actually needs.

Idle commits

Reserved capacity is a forecast of the year. The GPUs idle this week were paid for anyway.

Queues instead of prices

A fixed on-demand rate does not rise when a chip is scarce. The signal you get is a wait, or a region marked unavailable.

No public floor

An operator with a quiet pod rarely gets to post a price the whole market can see. Spare machines wait on a private thread.

02 — Matching

From a resting order to an allocation.

Three steps. Partial fills are a normal outcome.

01

Post

You send a side, an instrument, a size, a limit, a region, and a window. If the order does not cross, it rests where the book can see it.

02

Cross

The engine matches orders on the same shape when the bid is at or above the ask. A 128-GPU bid can fill against several smaller asks.

03

Allocate

Both sides receive the same record: what cleared, at what price, for which hour, in which region. Size that did not cross stays resting.

03 — Instruments

Hardware, named precisely.

A contract is a chip, a count, a region, a fabric, and a window. An instance and a bare-metal host can both rest in the book when those terms are explicit.

05 — Questions

Before you request a seat.

It is a venue. Providers list machines they operate. Buyers bid for a shape. When an order crosses, both sides get the same allocation record. You keep your own orchestration, image, and scheduler.

By the book. A bid and an ask cross when the shape matches and the bid is at or above the ask. The fill prints at that crossing price. There is no second rate card behind the book.

GPU clusters and smaller slices, plus CPU bare metal for the work around a training job. A listing names the hardware, the interconnect, the region, and the hours the machines are actually available.

The floor, the size, the region, and the window. Unfilled size stays resting. Capacity that was not posted is not allocated.

The record of a cross: instrument, size, price, region, and window. A large bid can complete against several asks. Anything that does not cross remains in the book.

Teams that buy bursts of GPU time, and operators with capacity that is not fully sold. Say which side you are on. Seats open in small cohorts.
